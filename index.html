The Museum

The museum was founded in the year 2026 by cultural scholar Philipp Gönitzer, who also acts as its director and curator. The building was designed by the lawyer and art patron Siegfried Rack. Several of El-Tetawy’s early films were produced there, including “1. Aufgabe”, “2 Aufgabe”, “Unicorn House”, and “Die Passion der Erdbeere”. Therefore the site carries a direct connection to the artist’s work and functions simultaneously as exhibition space and historical film location.

Collection and Program

The museum’s collection includes films and photographs by Amro El-Tetawy as well as works by artists whose positions resonate with his artistic approach, including Hermann Nitsch, Günter Brus, Otto Mühl, Alois Köchl, Gustav Januš, Elena Fekonja and Philipp fun Gonicer. The program includes solo and group exhibitions as well as film screenings, live music and performance.

Biography

Amro El-Tetawy was born on May 24, 2000, in Ain-Shams, Egypt. In the same year, his family emigrated to Austria, where he later lived primarily in Carinthia and Vienna. He attended Federal Secondary School Klagenfurt with a focus on music before enrolling at the Vienna Film School. During this period he produced several early short films including “1. Aufgabe”, “2 Aufgabe”, and “Unicorn House”. After refusing to submit “Unicorn House” as his diploma film—arguing that the faculty was not qualified to judge the work—he left the institution without graduating. His application to the Vienna Film Academy with these films resulted in a legal dispute after director Michael Haneke filed a complaint concerning the use of a swastika in two of the works. Following an arrest and investigation of El-Tetawy, the case was dismissed.

Curatorial Statement

The Amro El-Tetawy Museum seeks to bridge the independence of the underground art scene with the academic ambition of major institutions. Here, established artists encounter a curatorial punk rock attitude which ruthlessly places art and its aesthetic effects above ideology and prestige.

— Philipp Gönitzer

Visit

The Amro El-Tetawy Museum does not maintain regular public opening hours and its address is not publicly listed. Visitors are generally admitted during public events such as exhibitions, screenings, concerts, or performances. Outside of these occasions, visits are possible only by appointment. Once a visit has been confirmed, the address of the museum will be provided. Guided tours or film screenings from the collection may be arranged upon request. The museum reserves the right to decline visit requests.
